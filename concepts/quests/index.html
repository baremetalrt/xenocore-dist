    Quests¶

    All · 15 min read · No code · Last tested: never

    A quest is a data asset: a title, some objectives, a reward, and optional conditions for when it may be offered. The party's progress on every quest is held by one quest component on the player controller, in a ledger keyed by the quest asset itself. An NPC offers the quest through a Blueprint node, the ledger counts progress on its own, and the NPC's graph turns it in. The one idea that makes the rest make sense: the quest asset is the quest's identity. The ledger never asks which NPC, map or pawn is involved, so a quest keeps working when its giver is unloaded, the leader changes, or the game is saved and loaded.

    How it fits together¶

    Piece Kind Job
    Quests/DA_XenoCore_Quest_Collection01, DA_XenoCore_Quest_Monster01 Quest definitions (UXenoCoreQuestDefinition) The two Gym sample quests
    Quests/GaurPlain/DA_XenoCore_Quest_GP_* Quest definitions The seven quests of the Gaur Plain slice, given by NPCs placed in Maps/L_XenoCore_GaurPlain
    UXenoCoreQuestComponent Controller component The ledger: status, objective counts, who asked, which quest is tracked
    Offer Quest Blueprint latent node (UXenoCoreOfferQuest) Opens the offer screen, continues on Accepted or Declined
    UserInterface/HUD/W_XenoCore_QuestOffer Screen (UXenoCoreQuestOfferBase) Title, requester, summary, objectives, rewards, Accept/Decline
    UserInterface/W_XenoCore_QuestLog Screen (UXenoCoreQuestLogBase) Every accepted and finished quest, from the menu
    UserInterface/HUD/W_XenoCore_QuestTracker HUD plate (UXenoCoreQuestTrackerBase) The tracked quest and its outstanding objectives
    UserInterface/HUD/W_XenoCore_QuestReward HUD plate (UXenoCoreQuestRewardBase) The "quest complete" banner with what was paid
    UXenoCoreQuestUIStatics Blueprint function library Row info for the log, requester name, Open Quest Log

    The quest component is added to the player controller by the plugin's own game feature data, XenoCore (the XenoCore.uasset at the plugin's content root), so every XenoCore Experience has one. Blueprints reach it with Get Party Quests.

    The sample NPCs are in Maps/L_XenoCore_Gym: NPC_QuestGiver gives DA_XenoCore_Quest_Collection01 and NPC_MonsterQuestGiver gives DA_XenoCore_Quest_Monster01. Characters/NPC/B_XenoCore_Npc_Sample also carries Collection01, as a ready-made example; the plain B_XenoCore_Npc gives no quest.

    What the two sample quests do¶

    • Collection01 is a collect quest: one Collect Item objective on ID_XenoCore_EtherCrystal, rewarding ID_XenoCore_BlueChain plus gold and experience.

    • Monster01 is a kill quest: one Defeat Enemy objective on B_XenoCore_Minion_Melee, locked behind a Party Level At Least gate, rewarding ID_XenoCore_ScrapPlating plus gold and experience. Every minion killed while the quest is active counts, including any child Blueprint of the minion.

    Lifecycle¶

    A quest's status, shown on screen as the log's status column:

    Status Meaning Offerable?
    Unknown Never answered Yes
    Declined Turned down once Yes, the player can come back
    Active Accepted, objectives outstanding No
    Ready To Turn In Every objective met No
    Complete Turned in and paid. Final No
    1. Offer. The NPC's graph calls Offer Quest. If the quest is not offerable (already taken, or a gate does not hold) nothing opens. The screen pauses the conversation, which resumes on the answer. Exactly one of Accepted or Declined always fires, even if the screen is torn down.
    2. Accept. The status becomes Active and the counts are filled straight away from the bag. A collect quest for something you already carry can be Ready To Turn In the moment you accept it. The giver's name is recorded. If nothing is tracked yet, this quest becomes the tracked one.
    3. Progress.
      • Collect Item counts are recomputed from the bag every time the bag changes. They are never added up from pickups, so spending or selling the item lowers the count again.
      • Defeat Enemy counts go up by one per kill, only while the quest is Active. Kills before you accepted do not count. They cannot be recomputed, so they are what the save file keeps.
      • Talk To NPC counts when the party talks to the placed NPC whose Quest Id matches, before that NPC's own graph runs, so a last-step talk to the giver can hand in within the same conversation. Reach Location counts when the leader walks into the matching Quest Location (companions never count), once per visit for each quest.
      • When every objective is met the status moves to Ready To Turn In, and back to Active if a collected item leaves the bag.
    4. Turn in. The NPC's graph calls Turn In. It re-checks the bag first. If the quest is not really ready it returns false and stays Active. Otherwise it:
      • removes the collected items whose objective has Consume On Turn In ticked,
      • pays the gold, and experience to the whole roster (reserve members at their reduced rate, see Progression),
      • adds the reward items through the inventory statics,
      • marks the quest Complete, moves tracking to another running quest, and shows the reward banner.

    Gates are checked when a quest is offered, never again. A quest you already have is not taken away because the leader changed to a lower-level character.

    The quest marker ("!" bubble) over a giver shows while the quest is offerable or ready to turn in, and hides while it is Active. A gated quest shows no marker at all.

    Who asked. The offer screen and the tracker name the requester in this order: the quest's own Requester Name; otherwise the NPC offering it; otherwise the name recorded at Accept. The tracker has no NPC to ask, which is why the name is recorded. It reads "Return to name" when the quest is ready.

    The minimap marks what the tracked quest still wants in blue: the enemies a Defeat Enemy objective needs, the NPC of a Talk To NPC step, the Quest Location of a Reach Location step, and the giver once the quest is ready. See Minimap.

    The tracker follows exactly one quest. Pick it in the Quest Log. Show or hide it with the quest tracker button (see Controls).

    Saving. Every quest's status (by name), its counts, its recorded requester and the tracked quest are saved. On load, collect counts are recomputed from the restored bag; kill counts come from the file. See Save and front end.

    What you can change¶

    On a quest definition:

    Setting Where it lives What it does
    Title XenoCore › Quest The quest's name on every screen
    Kind XenoCore › Quest Collection, Material, Monster, Challenge, Search, Normal, Story. A label only: it titles the offer screen and sorts the log, and changes no behaviour
    Area XenoCore › Quest Free text shown beside the quest in the log
    Requester Name XenoCore › Quest Who is asking. Leave empty to use the NPC's Display Name
    Requester Portrait XenoCore › Quest The face on the offer screen. Empty hides the frame
    Summary XenoCore › Quest The paragraph under the requester
    Objectives XenoCore › Quest What must be done (below)
    Gates XenoCore › Quest Conditions before it is offered. All must hold. Empty means always offerable
    Reward XenoCore › Quest Gold, Experience, and Items (one entry per item, so ten of a thing is ten entries)
    Can Decline XenoCore › Quest Untick to show only Accept, and ignore Back

    Each objective:

    Setting What it does
    Description The prose line on the offer screen and in the log
    Counter Label The noun in "Name 0/2". Empty uses the item's own name
    Kind Collect Item, Defeat Enemy, Talk To NPC or Reach Location
    Item Target The item to hold (Collect Item)
    Enemy Target The enemy class to defeat (Defeat Enemy). A parent class counts every child. A soft reference: the quest does not load the enemy Blueprint
    Target Id Talk To NPC: the placed NPC's Quest Id. Reach Location: a placed XenoCore Quest Location's Location Id. Matched by name
    Required Count How many
    Consume On Turn In Take the items away on turn-in (Collect Item)
    Icon Source, Icon Category, Icon Texture The icon beside this objective on the offer screen, log and tracker

    Each gate is Party Level At Least (the leader's level) or Quest Completed (another quest must be turned in, not just accepted).

    Talk and location steps match by name

    A quest asset cannot point at an actor placed in a level, so Talk To NPC and Reach Location match the objective's Target Id against an NPC's Quest Id or a Quest Location's Location Id. Saving an objective with an empty Target Id reports an error. See Create a kill, talk or location quest.

    Fails silently: a gate that can never pass

    A quest whose gate never holds is simply never offered. There is no marker to notice missing, and nothing is logged. Get Gate Requirement Text on the quest component returns the reason as player text, but no screen shows it yet.

    Reordering objectives breaks existing saves

    Saved counts are stored in objective order. Reorder a shipped quest's Objectives and a player's saved kill counts land on the wrong objective.

    The screens' styling and spacing live on their Blueprints' class defaults under XenoCore › Quest: colours for the tracker and reward banner, the banner's duration, and so on.

    Extending it¶

    • A new quest is a new data asset, not code: duplicate DA_XenoCore_Quest_Collection01 into Content/MyGame/Quests/, edit it, and set it as the Quest of a placed NPC. See Make a collection quest.
    • Custom quest flow (extra lines, a cutscene between accept and the first objective) goes in the NPC's On Interact graph around Offer Quest and Turn In. Use Get Lines For with Greeting, Accepted, Declined, In Progress or Turn In to pick the NPC's authored lines.
    • Accepting from script (a story quest with no screen): call Accept on Get Party Quests. Leave Giver empty; the tracker then says "Return to the quest giver".
    • A new objective kind needs C++ in UXenoCoreQuestComponent: a listener for whatever event advances it, and the kind in EXenoCoreObjectiveKind. Append to the enum; never reorder it.
    • Reacting to quests from your own Blueprints: listen for XenoCore.Quest.Message.Changed and re-read the component, or XenoCore.Quest.Message.TurnedIn for the payout. See Events you can listen to.

    Console command for checking the ledger: XenoQuestStatus prints every quest the party has touched.

    Why it's built this way¶

    • On the controller, not a world subsystem. Quest state must outlive a map and be saved. The controller is also where Lyra and XenoCore keep other party-wide state, such as the bag.
    • Keyed by the definition. Same reason the progression component keys characters by their member definition: the NPC may be gone, and the asset outlives everything.
    • No "Offered" status. A state that exists only while a screen is up can be stranded by a crash or a level change. The screen's being open is the screen's business.
    • Counts recomputed from the bag. A running total drifts the first time anything changes the bag without the message it expects. Re-reading the bag is correct by construction.
    • Changed carries no detail; TurnedIn does. Every screen re-reads the component on Changed, so no copy can go stale. The turn-in payout is the exception: once paid, the gold is part of a running total and cannot be reconstructed by re-reading.
    • Kind is only a label. If code branched on it, adding a new kind would stop being data.
