    Give a map its music¶

    Level designer · 20 min · No code

    Result: your map plays a field track while the party explores, crossfades to a battle track the moment a fight starts, fades the battle track out under the victory fanfare when you win, and picks the field track up again from where it was cut.

    Before you start¶

    • Two looping tracks imported as Sound Waves (or Sound Cues or MetaSounds): one for exploring and one for battle. Either may be left out.
    • A map that runs the XenoCore Experience. See Build a world-partition map.

    How area music works¶

    One actor per map, XenoCore Area Music (AXenoCoreAreaMusic), holds both tracks. The class names no track of its own: you choose them on the placed actor, and an empty slot is silence. Each map's music lives and dies with that map, so it never carries into the next one.

    When What you hear
    The map starts Field Music fades in over Field Start Fade Seconds
    The party enters battle The field track fades out and Battle Music fades in, from the top, over Enter Battle Fade Seconds
    The battle is won The battle track fades out over Victory Fade Seconds, so the victory fanfare plays alone
    The battle ends without a win (you fled, or the enemy gave up) The battle track fades out over Leave Battle Fade Seconds
    The battle is over The field track fades back in over Field Return Fade Seconds, from where it was cut
    Night falls (19:00) or ends (05:00) Field Music Night, if set, crossfades with the day track over Day Night Fade Seconds; never mid-battle
    Rain or a storm starts The music lowers by the weather type's Music Volume Scale, over Weather Duck Seconds. See Tune a map's weather

    "In battle" is the same rule the rest of the template uses: the leader has drawn, or an enemy nearby is fighting the party. See Party. The actor checks it every Poll Seconds (0.2), so a transition can start up to a fifth of a second late.

    Both tracks play as UI sounds: the music carries on under the pause and party menus, which pause the world.

    Steps¶

    1. Put the tracks on Lyra's Music sound class¶

    1. Open each Sound Wave in the Content Browser.
    2. Under Sound, set Class to Lyra's Audio/Classes/Music.
    3. Tick Looping if the track is not already set to loop.
    4. Save.

    Fails silently: a track on the wrong sound class ignores the Music slider

    A track left on the default sound class plays at full volume whatever the player sets Music to in the options. Nothing warns. Put every music track on Audio/Classes/Music.

    2. Place the actor¶

    1. In Place Actors, search for XenoCore Area Music and drag it into your map. Where you put it does not matter: the music is not positional.
    2. In the Outliner, name it AreaMusic so it is easy to find.

    World Partition: keep it loaded everywhere

    In a partitioned map, the actor must not be spatially loaded, or it streams out with its cell and the music stops when the player walks away from wherever you dropped it. A newly placed Area Music actor already has Is Spatially Loaded off. Leave it off.

    3. Choose the tracks¶

    Select the actor and, in its Details panel:

    Setting Category Value
    Field Music Music › Field Your exploring track. Empty = Fallback Field Music from Project Settings › Game › XenoCore Content › Startup, or silence if that is empty too
    Field Volume Music › Field Start at 1.0; set it in step 4
    Battle Music Music › Battle Your battle track. Empty = Fallback Battle Music from the same settings; if that is empty too, the field track plays straight through fights
    Battle Volume Music › Battle Start at 1.0; set it in step 4
    Resume Field Music Music › Field On (default): after a battle the field track continues from where it was cut. Off: from the top
    Field Music Night Music › Field Optional. Played while exploring at night. Empty = the field track plays at night too
    Field Night Volume Music › Field Volume of the night track

    The battle track always starts from the top.

    Resume needs a Sound Wave

    The field track resumes from where it was cut only if it is a Sound Wave. A Sound Cue or MetaSound restarts from the top after each battle.

    4. Match the two volumes¶

    Two tracks bought from different places are rarely mastered to the same loudness. Left at equal volumes, every fight jumps up or down in level.

    1. Measure each file's average loudness (RMS, in dB) in any audio tool. In the free editor Audacity, open the file, select all, and choose Analyze › Measure RMS.
    2. Pick a target loudness and set each volume to bring its track there. Every 6 dB louder than the target is roughly half the volume. For example, a battle track 7 dB louder than the field track needs a Battle Volume a little under half its Field Volume.
    3. Play, and adjust both volumes together by ear. Scaling both by the same amount keeps the match.

    The sample map Maps/L_XenoCore_GaurPlain has an AreaMusic actor with both track slots empty: the template ships no licensed music. Set your own tracks on it, or set Fallback Field Music and Fallback Battle Music in Project Settings › Game › XenoCore Content › Startup to give every map with empty slots the same music.

    5. Tune the fades (optional)¶

    All under Music › Transitions, in seconds:

    Setting Default Fades
    Enter Battle Fade Seconds 1.0 Field out and battle in when a fight starts
    Leave Battle Fade Seconds 2.5 Battle out when a fight ends without a win
    Victory Fade Seconds 0.75 Battle out on a win, under the fanfare
    Field Return Fade Seconds 3.0 Field back in after the battle
    Day Night Fade Seconds 5.0 Day and night field tracks into each other
    Weather Duck Seconds 4.0 The music following a weather change in volume
    Poll Seconds (advanced) 0.2 How often the battle state is read

    Field Start Fade Seconds (3.0, Music › Field) is the fade-in when the map starts.

    Save the level.

    Test it¶

    1. Press Play in your map. The field track fades in over about three seconds.
    2. Open the pause menu. The music keeps playing.
    3. Hit an enemy. Within a second the field track gives way to the battle track.
    4. Win the fight. The battle track fades away quickly and the victory fanfare plays on its own; the field track then returns from the point where it was cut, not from the start.
    5. Start another fight and run until the enemy gives up. The battle track fades out more slowly and the field track returns.
    6. In the options menu, lower Music. Both tracks get quieter.

    The Output Log prints one line when the map starts, naming each track and its volume: AreaMusic <name>: field=... (x1.00), night=... (x1.00), battle=... (x1.00). <name> is the actor's internal object name, not its Outliner label, so the two can differ.

    If it doesn't work¶

    Fails silently: no music at all

    There is no Area Music actor in the map, or Field Music is empty and so is Fallback Field Music in Project Settings (as in the shipped Gaur Plain). Check the log line above: no line means no actor; field=None means an empty slot.

    The music stops when you walk away

    The actor is spatially loaded in a World Partition map. Select it and untick Is Spatially Loaded.

    Music from another map keeps playing, or two tracks play at once

    Two Area Music actors are in the same map, or one is in a sublevel that loads alongside it. Keep exactly one per map.

    Fights get much louder (or quieter) than exploring

    The two volumes are not loudness-matched. Redo step 4.

    The battle track never starts

    Battle Music is empty, which by design keeps the field track playing through fights. If it is set, check that the party really enters battle: lock on and draw, or let an enemy hit you.

    The Music slider does nothing

    The tracks are not on Audio/Classes/Music. See step 1.

    More in World and maps troubleshooting.
