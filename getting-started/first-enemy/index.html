    Your first enemy¶

    Designer · 30 min · No code · Last tested: never

    Result: a new enemy with its own name and level. It fights with a Break and a Topple Art and always drops a gold chest. You place it in the gym map and fight it.

    Before you start¶

    • Tour of the project. You should know where the XenoCore content folder is.

    How an enemy is put together¶

    An enemy is one Blueprint plus a few assets it points at:

    Piece Sample asset What it decides
    Enemy Blueprint Characters/Enemies/Minion/B_XenoCore_Minion_Melee Name, level, rewards, loot, death, mesh
    Pawn data PawnData_XenoCore_Minion_Melee Which abilities it has: attack, hit reaction, knock-downs, death
    Arts Minion/Arts/DA_XenoCore_Minion_Rend, DA_XenoCore_Minion_Sweep The Arts it uses in a fight, listed on the Blueprint's Arts
    Stats effect Battle/Attributes/GE_XenoCore_BaseStats_Minion_Melee Its stats at its level, read from a curve table
    Drop table Inventory/Loot/DA_XenoCore_Drops_Minion What its chests can hold
    AI BP_AIC_XenoCore_Enemy + ST_XenoCore_EnemyFreeroam How it patrols, engages, chases, gives up, and picks Arts

    The AI is shared by every enemy, so a new enemy needs none of its own. You will make a child of the minion Blueprint and change only what makes this enemy different.

    Steps¶

    1. Make a folder for your own content¶

    Put your assets in your own folder, not inside XenoCore's. Template updates replace XenoCore's folders, and your work should survive them.

    1. In the Content Browser, go to Content and create a folder, for example MyGame/Enemies.

    2. Make a child of the minion¶

    1. In the Content Browser, go to XenoCore Content › Characters › Enemies › Minion (under Plugins; the folder may read just XenoCore). If it is missing, tick Plugin Content in the Content Browser's Settings; see Tour of the project.
    2. Right-click B_XenoCore_Minion_Melee and choose Create Child Blueprint Class. Name it B_MyEnemy.
    3. Drag B_MyEnemy into MyGame/Enemies and choose Move Here.

    A child starts identical to its parent and keeps up with it: anything you do not change still comes from the minion. The seven sample enemy types in Minion/Variants are made exactly this way.

    A child of the melee minion, not of the enemy base

    B_XenoCore_Enemy_Base is the parent every enemy shares, and it has no mesh, stats or abilities. The melee minion is the complete working example. Duplicate data assets (pawn data, ability sets, stats effects) when you need your own; make Blueprints as children.

    3. Give it a name and a level¶

    1. Open B_MyEnemy and click Class Defaults.
    2. Under XenoCore › Enemy, set:
      • Display Name to Stonejaw. This appears on the target badge when you lock on.
      • Enemy Level to 8. The level scales every stat through the curve table, so this alone makes it tougher than the low-level sample minions.
      • Experience Reward to 30, double the melee minion's 15. This is the base amount: the inherited Experience Level Scale row multiplies it by the enemy's level, and the level-difference adjustment is applied after that.
    3. Compile and Save.

    Keep enemies within reach of the party's level

    An enemy far below the party's level never notices the party: it does not start a fight by sight, sound or ether, though it still fights back when attacked. That is the Ignore When Levels Below setting (6 levels by default) on the AI controller every enemy shares, Characters/Enemies/Core/BP_AIC_XenoCore_Enemy › Class Defaults › XenoCore › AI, and it is intended. If your enemy won't start a fight, check its level first.

    4. Make it drop a gold chest¶

    1. In Class Defaults, under XenoCore › Loot, tick Always Gold Chest.
    2. Leave Drop Table as the minion's DA_XenoCore_Drops_Minion for now. The seven sample enemy types each use their family's table instead (DA_XenoCore_Drops_Grunt, _Skirmisher, _Bulwark, _Brute), with level bands on their entries.
    3. Compile and Save.

    Chests hold a drop-table reference and a tier, and roll their contents when they are opened, not when they drop. Designing your own drop table is covered in a recipe.

    5. Place it¶

    1. Open the map Maps/L_XenoCore_Gym. The sample minions are already placed here.
    2. Drag B_MyEnemy from the Content Browser into the level, on the floor, away from the other minions so you can fight it alone.
    3. Save the level.

    Click the placed enemy in the viewport: its Details panel shows the same XenoCore settings, and a value changed there affects only that one placement. Display Name, Enemy Level, Experience Reward and Always Gold Chest are among them, and so are its Patrol Points (XenoCore › AI › Patrol), which you can drag in the viewport. That is how one enemy Blueprint becomes many different monsters in a map. For a squad that wanders, fights together and comes back after you defeat it, place a XenoCore Enemy Spawner instead: see Place enemies.

    Test it¶

    1. Press Play.
    2. Walk up to your enemy and lock on to it (pad RB, keyboard Tab). The target badge reads Stonejaw.
    3. Attack it. Whether an enemy attacks first depends on its Detection (XenoCore › AI › Detection): None (passive) waits to be hit, Sight, Sound and Ether start the fight themselves. Try changing it on the placed enemy.
    4. Fight it. It auto-attacks and uses its Arts: Rend, which Breaks, then Sweep, which Topples the Broken target. If a companion hits it harder than you, it turns on the companion: the red ring under its target moves with it.
    5. Defeat it. After about two seconds a gold chest appears where it fell. Open it.

    If it doesn't work¶

    It takes no damage and cannot die

    Enemy Pawn Data is empty. Death and damage come from the abilities that pawn data grants, so without it the enemy is invulnerable. An error naming the enemy is logged. Set it back to PawnData_XenoCore_Minion_Melee.

    Its stats double up after a level change

    If you make your own stats effect, every modifier in it must use Override, not Add. Otherwise re-applying the effect stacks the numbers on top of the old ones.

    The chest opens empty

    The drop table has no pool for that chest tier (Wooden, Silver or Gold). Every tier a chest can roll needs its own pool. A warning is logged.

    Fails silently: it never uses its Arts

    An enemy uses only the Arts on its Blueprint's Arts list (XenoCore › Enemy › Arts). A child of the minion keeps Rend and Sweep; if the list is empty, the enemy only auto-attacks and nothing is logged.

    It never starts a fight on its own

    Its Detection is None (passive), or it is 6 or more levels below your party leader (see the tip in step 3). Hit it first, or change Detection on the placed enemy.

    You now have¶

    Your own enemy, placed in a map and fought. You also know which settings make one Blueprint into many monsters.
